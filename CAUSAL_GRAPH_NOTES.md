# EconoCausal: Causal Graph and Assumptions



## 1. Objective



This document describes the initial conceptual Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) for EconoCausal. The graph represents hypothesized relationships between discount assignment and customer purchases.



## 2. Treatment and Outcome



- **Treatment:** `discount` — the discount level offered to a customer.

- **Outcome:** `purchase` — whether the customer made a purchase.

- **Potential confounders:** `price`, `competitor_price`, `promotion`, and `season`.



## 3. Hypothesized Relationships



The initial graph contains the following relationships:



- `price` → `discount`

- `price` → `purchase`

- `competitor_price` → `discount`

- `competitor_price` → `purchase`

- `promotion` → `discount`

- `promotion` → `purchase`

- `season` → `discount`

- `season` → `purchase`

- `discount` → `purchase`



These arrows represent hypotheses that require business justification and validation. They are not established causal relationships merely because they appear in the graph.



## 4. Potential Confounding



Price, competitor pricing, promotions, and season may influence both the discount offered and customer purchasing behavior. If these factors are genuine common causes of treatment and outcome, failing to account for them could bias an estimated discount effect.



The dataset alone cannot establish that all confounders have been measured. Other factors, such as customer preferences, income, purchase history, and marketing targeting rules, may also affect discount assignment and purchasing behavior.



## 5. Identification Assumptions



A causal interpretation of the estimated discount effect would require assumptions such as:



1. **Conditional exchangeability:** After controlling for sufficient relevant variables, discount assignment is independent of the potential purchase outcomes.

2. **Positivity:** Relevant customer groups have a nonzero probability of receiving the discount levels being compared.

3. **Consistency:** The observed outcome corresponds to the treatment level actually received, with treatment levels defined consistently.

4. **Correct temporal ordering:** The assumed causes precede discount assignment and purchase.

5. **Adequate measurement and modeling:** Relevant variables are measured sufficiently well, and the estimation procedure is appropriate for the data.



These assumptions have not yet been verified.



## 6. Limitations



The current graph is conceptual. It does not prove that hidden confounding has been eliminated or that the estimated treatment effect is causal.



The existing dataset is synthetic, so findings should not be treated as evidence of real-world customer behavior or guaranteed business outcomes.
